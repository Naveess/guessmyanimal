## Overall Impression

The logic underneath this surface is some of the most careful engineering in the codebase — the chat-status fix, the OBS-aware confirm dialog, the honest OAuth consent screen are all evidence of someone who has actually run this live and fixed real failures. But the visual skin wrapping that logic is a second, undocumented design language that violates two of DESIGN.md's own Named Rules at scale, on the two screens a streamer is most likely to have on camera. The biggest opportunity here isn't inventing anything new — it's deleting the arcade skin's shadow/glow/motion layer and letting the calm, trustworthy system the rest of the site already has do the work DESIGN.md says it should.

## What's Working

1. **The chat-status three-state fix** (`.streamer-chatstatus.is-waiting`, streamer.js ~577–647) — a named correction of a real prior bug where a mistyped channel used to read as permanently connected. Specific to this product's own incident history, not a generic status pill.
2. **The in-page two-step End Stream confirm** (`#streamerEndConfirm`, streamer.js ~782–808), built because a native `window.confirm()` can silently fail to render inside OBS's Browser Source — reasoning that could only come from someone who tested this in an actual streaming setup.
3. **The `PLATFORMS` data-array pattern** (streamer.js ~874–906) — the live Connect button and inert "Coming soon" rows render from one array, so adding a second platform is one entry plus a functions pair, never a redesign of the screen.

## Priority Issues

**[P0] The arcade skin's border+shadow+glow treatment breaks two of DESIGN.md's own Named Rules at once**
- **Why it matters:** The Border-Or-Shadow Rule and the "don't reach for a glow" line in Do's and Don'ts are two of the handful of rules CLAUDE.md calls out as most likely to matter to any change — and they're being violated systemically, not as an edge case, across `.streamer-point` (style.css ~4649–4652), `#streamerDashboard .party-stats` (~5002–5007), `.party-hintcard` (~5067–5072), `.streamer-board` (~5160–5166), all three `.streamer-consent-*` panels (~5581–5680), and the five `--arc-*glow` tokens consumed from line ~4560 through ~5790. The detector independently flagged the same shape 19 times.
- **Fix:** Pick one per element — drop the manufactured shadow and keep the flush `--line` border (consistent with every other card on the site), or go fully floating on `--shadow-l` and drop the border. Replace the five `--arc-*glow` custom properties with the standard elevation tokens already used everywhere else in dark mode.
- **Suggested command:** `/impeccable polish`

**[P1] Pre-connect "how it works" content is missing — a real content gap, not just a style one**
- **What:** DESIGN.md's Streamer-surface section describes "three reassurance points and a 3-step how-it-works... shar[ing] one drawn-icon badge (`.streamer-step-icon`)." The CSS for this still exists (style.css ~4384–4402) but streamer.html has zero matching markup — it's dead code, and the feature it styled has quietly disappeared. The only stand-in, `.streamer-stage`, is marked `aria-hidden="true"`.
- **Why it matters:** A first-time streamer — and every screen-reader user, unconditionally — must decide whether to grant Twitch OAuth access before learning the actual mechanic (hints unlock on a timer, first correct guess wins). That's a trust-sensitive decision made with less information than the product's own design spec intends.
- **Fix:** Restore an accessible how-it-works block before the Connect button, reusing the existing CSS instead of writing new.
- **Suggested command:** `/impeccable clarify` (content/copy gap) or `/impeccable harden` (accessibility gap)

**[P1] Animation budget overrun — 6 decorative loops against a site-wide cap of 2**
- **What:** DESIGN.md caps the entire site at two authored animation moments. This one surface alone adds `streamerFloat`/`streamerFloatAlt` (bubble drift), `streamerGlow` (card pulse), `streamerChev` (chevron blink), `streamerSweep` (button marquee), and `streamerTense` (digit pulse) — style.css ~4863–4877, ~5254–5263.
- **Why it matters:** Directly contradicts "everything else is quiet, functional feedback," and is genuinely distracting on a surface meant to be captured live in front of an audience — the opposite of what a broadcast tool should ask of its own chrome.
- **Fix:** Keep the functional escalation (the timer rail's color/label changes) and cut the purely decorative loops. Credit due: all of it is already correctly gated behind `prefers-reduced-motion` (~4880–4886, ~5267–5271), so the fix is deletion, not new accessibility work.
- **Suggested command:** `/impeccable quieter`

**[P2] A 22px radius forms an undocumented parallel scale step**
- **What:** `border-radius: 22px` recurs 13+ times across the arcade panels; the DESIGN.md ladder jumps from 20px (`--r-card`) to 26px (`--r-photo`) with nothing between. Buttons on this surface (`.streamer-connectbtn`, `#streamerGuess`, `.party-hintbtn`, `#streamerNext`) separately use 16px, the ladder's "floating panel" role, not the 14px "Button" role.
- **Why it matters:** Consistent-but-undocumented is still a fork: the next person touching this surface has no way to know 22px is intentional rather than a typo, and every other radius decision on the site is traceable to a documented role.
- **Fix:** Either snap these panels to `--r-card` (20px) and buttons to 14px, or add 22px to DESIGN.md's ladder as a named, deliberate step the way the Mystery Animal presentation scale is documented as an exception.
- **Suggested command:** `/impeccable document` (record the exception) or `/impeccable polish` (conform to the existing ladder)

**[P3] Copy over-relies on em-dashes, diluting the site's "plain-spoken" voice**
- **What:** The detector flagged `em-dash-overuse`; raw count is 19 em-dashes in streamer.html, concentrated in the pitch and consent copy.
- **Why it matters:** PRODUCT.md states the brand tone as "plain-spoken, a little wry." A single punctuation mark doing this much structural work in the copy reads as a stylistic tic rather than a considered voice, especially concentrated on the exact screen meant to build trust before an OAuth handshake.
- **Fix:** Rewrite a portion of the flagged sentences with periods or commas; keep em-dashes for genuine asides, not as the default connector.
- **Suggested command:** `/impeccable clarify`

## Persona Red Flags

**Jordan (First-Timer):** Lands on `#streamerConnect` and has to decide whether to grant Twitch OAuth access based on a hero headline, three one-line reassurance cards, and an `aria-hidden` decorative mockup — no explicit mechanic explanation exists until *after* they've already connected (see P1 above). The loud arcade motion on this exact screen works against the trust job the very next screen (`#streamerConsent`) is carefully built to do.

**Sam (Accessibility-Dependent):** `.streamer-stage` is entirely `aria-hidden="true"`, and since the how-it-works steps markup doesn't exist either, a screen-reader user gets *zero* explanation of round mechanics before the OAuth screen — worse off than a sighted first-timer, who at least sees the decorative mockup. Counterpoint in Sam's favor: every decorative animation is correctly stripped under `prefers-reduced-motion`.

**Riley (Stress-Tester):** Mostly handled well — long usernames wrap (`overflow-wrap: anywhere` on `.streamer-board-name`), 100+ guessers truncate to an honest "top 8 of N," 0 guessers gets explicit empty copy, expired rounds freeze the timer (`frozenTimerMs`) rather than drifting negative, and a dropped IRC connection reconnects with backoff and visible status. One open question: chat guesses are deliberately not single-flighted (to avoid dropping simultaneous correct guesses), and nothing throttles POST volume to `/api/party/guess` beyond the browser's own per-origin connection limit — worth an actual load test at high concurrent chat volume before this leaves beta.

2. The full-bleed `.streamer-timerbar`, the `.mystery-stat` timer digit, and that digit's own "tense" pulse are three simultaneous visual channels for one number — more noise than a compressed stream benefits from.

4. `.streamer-textbtn` "Disconnect" sits immediately next to the "Playing as X" success line — a low-consequence-looking label right next to a genuinely destructive action, an easy misclick right after a positive moment.
5. `streamer.js?v=20260914-1` and `style.css?v=20260915-2` look internally consistent with the site's cache-busting discipline, but should be re-verified against whichever change actually produced the current arcade CSS, to confirm the version was bumped for it specifically.
