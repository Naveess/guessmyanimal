## Design Specificity Verdict

**LLM assessment**: Grounded, but unevenly. DESIGN.md is upfront that the shell — full-bleed brand splash → photo card → scroll-into-facts — is borrowed wholesale from Bumble, and structurally that's visible: strip the copy and this could be a recipe app or a dating profile. What rescues it is the writing layer: Quick Answers are phrased as the literal question a player asks out loud, pills state the answer in words before colour, the starter chips are curated oddities (Octopus, Sloth, Platypus, Axolotl, Hyena, Pangolin) rather than dog/cat filler, and the fact card is written as ammunition to drop back into the conversation. The menu, share panel, and report dialog, by contrast, read as close to interchangeable utility boilerplate — nothing there signals "animal cheat sheet" specifically. Specific where it counts (the moment of answering), generic in the scaffolding around it.

**Deterministic scan**: `detect.mjs --json index.html` returned clean (`[]`, exit 0) after the project's existing sanctioned-ignore config. Browser-injected detection found one live finding on the animal-detail view, reproduced identically across 3 runs: `low-contrast: 1.2:1 (need 4.5:1) — text #ffce1f on #e4e6da`, on `.pcard-sub` (the "Mammal · North America" kicker under the animal name).

**This is a false positive, and I've now visually confirmed it directly against a rendered screenshot.** `#e4e6da` is `.pcard`'s CSS-declared placeholder background-color, not what's behind the text once the photo has loaded — the element sits over the photo and its `.pcard-scrim` gradient, with a `text-shadow: 0 1px 10px rgba(0,0,0,.5)` for legibility, exactly like the sibling `.pcard-name` treatment. In the actual desktop and mobile screenshots the yellow kicker text reads clearly against the dark scrim. The static CLI scanner can't see this because it never renders the page; the browser-injected check ran the same flat two-color formula against a background colour that's never actually visible once a photo loads. No fix needed — but worth a scoped `low-contrast` ignore in `.impeccable/config.json` for `.pcard-sub` so this doesn't re-flag on every future hook run (say the word and I'll add it).

## Overall Impression

The product's central idea is sharply executed: the Quick Answers list, the photo-card reveal, and the fact card together deliver on "an answer in four seconds" better than almost anything currently *around* that core — the menu, the report flow, the ad-slot placeholder, and the empty first-run desktop composition all read as unfinished edges on an otherwise confident centre. The single biggest opportunity is closing that gap: the parts of the product a player actually touches mid-game are excellent; the parts framing them still read as scaffolding.

## What's Working

- **The `.pcard` reveal** (`deal` keyframe, style.css:158–169): the clip-path top-down uncover plus a blurred/saturated `.pcard-bg` filling a portrait frame behind a `contain`-fit photo solves "Wikipedia photos are mostly landscape" cleanly — confirmed in the Wolf screenshots, no letterboxing, no cropped-out animal.
- **The Quick Answers pill system**: every value states the answer in words first ("Can be," "Sometimes," "In places") with colour layered on top, never colour alone — the clearest translation of the product's "honest about uncertainty" principle into an actual UI decision.
- **Starter chips** (Octopus/Sloth/Platypus/Axolotl/Hyena/Pangolin): a small, deliberate brand choice that signals "broad, weird coverage" the instant the page loads, rather than a generic "type here" prompt.

## Priority Issues

**[P1] The extra-shot media pipeline can surface a painted illustration as if it were a photo.**
- **Why it matters**: On the Wolf page, one extra shot is captioned "Life restoration of Canis mosbachensis, the wolf's immediate ancestor" — paleoart, not a photograph — rendered with the identical shadow/frame treatment as a real photo, no visual distinction. PRODUCT.md names photo integrity as a specific, guarded commitment (it calls out a-z-animals stock photos as a banned source by name); a player citing "look, it's got four legs, right here" from a painting is exactly the confident-wrong-answer moment this product exists to prevent.
- **Fix**: The `JUNK_CAPTION` filter in app.js (line 254) already excludes "diagram/map/skeleton/illustration/drawing" captions — extend the pattern to catch "life restoration" and "reconstruction," two very common Wikipedia caption phrasings for paleoart that currently slip through.
- **Suggested command**: `/impeccable harden`

**[P1] The empty ad-slot placeholder ships visible, unlabeled, and permanently empty.**
- **Why it matters**: `.adslot` (style.css:563) renders a 100px dashed-border box on every single animal page today, confirmed in every screenshot, with ads "not yet implemented" per PRODUCT.md. It carries no "Ad space" label — nothing distinguishes it from a broken or missing component on a product whose whole design language otherwise reads as intentional and finished. It also sits directly beneath the fact card, the product's one confirmed emotional peak, undercutting the ending.
- **Fix**: Collapse it to zero height (or `display:none`) until a real ad exists; reserve the layout space via a JS-measured swap-in only once something actually fills it, rather than shipping a visible placeholder to every real user today.
- **Suggested command**: `/impeccable polish`

**[P2] The home screen's first decision point exceeds the product's own minimal-choice standard.**
- **Why it matters**: Search field + 6 starter chips + dice button = 7 simultaneous paths on the one screen the product is proudest of keeping simple. Confirmed via the cognitive-load checklist (below) and visually on the desktop screenshot, where all 7 are visible at once with no grouping.
- **Fix**: Either fold the starter chips behind a single "Try one of these" reveal, or cut the set from 6 to 3–4 so it reads as a hint rather than a second full menu of options.
- **Suggested command**: `/impeccable distill`

**[P2] No fast path back to recently-viewed animals, despite the code anticipating that exact need.**
- **Why it matters**: app.js's own comment on the Wikipedia cache (line 232) says "during a game you flick back and forth between the same few animals" — but the UI gives no recency list, only the raw search box. A player settling "wait, was the wolf one nocturnal or the fox?" has to re-type from scratch every time, mid-conversation.
- **Fix**: A small recent-lookups row (last 3–4) replacing or supplementing the starter chips once a player has looked something up, reusing the existing chip component.
- **Suggested command**: `/impeccable delight`

**[P3] The report dialog carries none of the brand's stated voice at its highest-stakes moment.**
- **Why it matters**: PRODUCT.md's Brand Commitments explicitly describe the site's tone as "plain-spoken, a little wry, honest about the site's limits" — but `#reportDlg` is a bare, clinical form with no line acknowledging that. Reporting a problem is a genuinely vulnerable interaction (the user is telling you the site failed them); the tone gap is most visible exactly where it matters most.
- **Fix**: One sentence under the dialog title — something like "Some of our judgement calls are arguable — tell us and we'll take a look" — costs nothing and matches the established voice.
- **Suggested command**: `/impeccable clarify`

## Persona Red Flags

**Alex (impatient power user, repeat lookups within one game)**: The dice button and the loud `.btn-solid#another` "Another animal" CTA are the only reroll mechanisms — both are *random*, not *back-to-an-animal-I-just-saw*. Combined with no recency list (P2 above), Alex pays a full re-type cost every time the group circles back to an animal already looked up minutes earlier in the same session.

**Jordan (confused first-timer)**: The dice button (`#random`) is a bare die-face icon with only an `aria-label`, no visible text — no discoverable meaning until pressed. Separately, on the Wolf page "What is it?" renders as a plain grey pill while "Carnivore?" renders green right beside it (visually confirmed) — Jordan, who hasn't read DESIGN.md's "colour is a second signal" rule, has no way to know why some pills are colourful and others aren't.

**Casey (distracted mobile user, phone in hand mid-conversation)**: The solid-yellow "Another animal" button is the single most visually prominent element in the actions row (confirmed in both mobile screenshots — filled Sun, everything else outline), sitting directly beside "Report a problem" and "Share." A distracted thumb reaching for Share is one mis-tap from discarding the very animal the group is mid-discussion about, with no undo.

## Minor Observations

- The native search input's clear button (`×`) renders in the OS-default blue accent in Chromium/Edge — an unthemed system control breaking the "no cold, un-authored colour" rule.
- On desktop, the home screen's composition doesn't scale up — confirmed visually, a large flat field of yellow surrounds a phone-width column at 1280px. Not broken, just an unused opportunity at wider viewports.
- `.factcard`'s solid-yellow fill, the dice button, and (once shipped) a solid CTA in the actions row put up to three Sun-coloured touchpoints in a single scroll pass on the animal page — worth checking against DESIGN.md's "One-Yellow Rule" now that the system is documented.
- Dark-mode wordmark showed a possible double-stroke/ghosting artifact on the bold Onest weight in the headless screenshot — may be a rendering artifact rather than a real bug; worth a quick real-device glance given how load-bearing that headline is.

## Questions to Consider

1. If speed is the entire premise, why does "Dangerous?" — arguably the single most game-relevant, adrenaline-loaded question — sit unranked in row three instead of being promoted or visually weighted above "Hibernates?" and "Domesticated?"
2. The engineering already anticipates users flicking back and forth between the same few animals — what would the home screen look like if it took that seriously instead of treating every lookup as a first-time search?
3. Is an empty dashed box really the right way to look "ad-ready" before there's an ad, or would the system's own "considered, not decorated" principle be better served by reserving the space invisibly?
