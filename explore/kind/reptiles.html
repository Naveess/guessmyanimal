    Reptiles

    Snakes, lizards, crocodilians, turtles and tortoises, plus the tuatara, which looks like a lizard but belongs to a group of its own.

    The slow worm is here too. It looks like a snake, but it's a legless lizard.

    32 animals, A to Z.

    • 🐊AlligatorTwo species of broad-snouted crocodilian: the American alligator of the south-eastern US, and the much smaller Chinese alligator, which is critically endangered.
    • 🐍AnacondaLarge, semiaquatic boas of tropical South America.
    • 🦎Basilisk lizardFour species of large lizards from the rainforests of southern Mexico, Central America and northern South America.
    • 🦎Bearded dragonEight species of Australian lizards named for the spiny "beard" under the throat, which darkens and puffs up when they are stressed, threatened or courting.
    • 🐍Boa constrictorA large, heavy-bodied, non-venomous snake of tropical America, and a staple of pet collections.
    • 🐍BoomslangA highly venomous tree snake of sub-Saharan Africa; its name is Afrikaans for "tree snake".
    • 🐊CaimanSix species of alligator relatives from Central and South America, living in marshes, swamps, lakes and mangrove rivers and fairly nocturnal.
    • 🦎ChameleonAbout 200 species of Old World lizard with independently moving eyes, a projectile tongue and gripping feet and tail.
    • 🐍CobraVenomous snakes that rear up and spread a hood when threatened.
    • 🐍CopperheadA heavy-bodied pit viper of the eastern United States, camouflaged by dark hourglass bands on a copper to brown background.
    • 🐊CrocodileBig, armoured reptiles that ambush prey at the water’s edge across the tropics, from the 1.5 m dwarf crocodile to the saltwater crocodile, which can pass 6 m.
    • 🦎GeckoNearly 2,000 species of small, mostly nocturnal lizards, the most species-rich lizard group.
    • 🐾GharialA crocodilian of the rivers of the northern Indian subcontinent, and among the longest of all, with a very long, narrow snout and 110 interlocking teeth built for catching fish.
    • 🐍Grass snakeA non-venomous, semi-aquatic Eurasian snake, usually dark green or brown with a yellow or white collar behind the head.
    • 🦎IguanaLarge plant-eating lizards of tropical America, with a dewlap and a crest of long scales down the back.
    • 🦎Komodo dragonThe largest living lizard, a monitor lizard found only on a few Indonesian islands.
    • 🦎LizardScaly reptiles, usually with four legs, with over 7,000 species.
    • 🐍MambaFour species of fast, highly venomous African snakes in the cobra family.
    • 🦎Monitor lizardAbout 94 species of lizards with long necks, powerful tails and claws and strong legs, from 20 cm long to the 3 m Komodo dragon.
    • 🐍PythonA family of 39 non-venomous snakes of Africa, Asia and Australia, including some of the largest snakes in the world.
    • 🐍RattlesnakeThirty-six species of venomous pit vipers of the Americas, named for the rattle of interlocking keratin segments at the tail tip.
    • 🐢Sea turtleSeven species of ocean-going turtles, including the green, loggerhead, hawksbill and leatherback.
    • 🐍SidewinderA small rattlesnake of the deserts of the southwestern US and northwestern Mexico.
    • 🦎SkinkOne of the most diverse lizard families, with more than 1,500 species.
    • 🐾Slow wormA legless lizard native to Europe: neither a worm nor a snake.
    • 🐍SnakeLimbless reptiles with more than 4,170 species, found on every continent except Antarctica.
    • 🐍TaipanThree species of large, fast, extremely venomous snakes from Australia and New Guinea, in the cobra family.
    • 🐢TerrapinTerrapins are turtles of fresh or brackish water with clawed feet rather than flippers.
    • 🐢TortoiseLand-living turtles with domed shells and column-like, elephant-like legs.
    • 🐾TuataraA lizard-like reptile found only in New Zealand, and the last survivor of the order Rhynchocephalia, the dominant group of small reptiles in the Jurassic.
    • 🐢TurtleShelled reptiles, about 360 species, including land-living tortoises, freshwater terrapins and sea turtles.
    • 🐍ViperVenomous snakes with long, hinged fangs that fold back against the roof of the mouth when not in use.
