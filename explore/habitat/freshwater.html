    River and lake animals

    Animals of fresh water: fish like trout, carp, piranhas and catfish, and the animals that live in and around rivers and lakes, such as otters, beavers, hippos, platypuses and kingfishers.

    It includes a few you might not expect, such as river dolphins, freshwater crabs and the axolotl, which spends its whole life underwater.

    64 animals, A to Z.

    • 🐊AlligatorTwo species of broad-snouted crocodilian: the American alligator of the south-eastern US, and the much smaller Chinese alligator, which is critically endangered.
    • 🐍AnacondaLarge, semiaquatic boas of tropical South America.
    • 🐠AngelfishThree species of tall, flat, freshwater cichlids from the Amazon, the Orinoco and rivers of the Guiana Shield in South America.
    • 🦎AxolotlA Mexican salamander that never goes through metamorphosis: adults keep their feathery external gills and stay in the water.
    • 🦎Basilisk lizardFour species of large lizards from the rainforests of southern Mexico, Central America and northern South America.
    • 🐟BassThe largemouth bass, an olive-green freshwater fish with a dark, jagged stripe along each side, native to the eastern and central US, south-eastern Canada and northern Mexico.
    • 🦫BeaverLarge semiaquatic rodents, the second-largest living rodents after capybaras.
    • 🪲BeetleInsects whose front wings are hardened into wing-cases.
    • 🐸BullfrogThe largest true frog in North America, named for the male’s call, which sounds like a bull bellowing.
    • 🐊CaimanSix species of alligator relatives from Central and South America, living in marshes, swamps, lakes and mangrove rivers and fairly nocturnal.
    • 🐾CapybaraThe largest living rodent, a semiaquatic grazer found in every South American country except Chile.
    • 🐟CarpThe common carp is a large freshwater fish of lakes and rivers in Europe and Asia.
    • 🐟CatfishA huge, varied order of fish named for their whisker-like barbels.
    • 🐾CormorantAbout 40 species of dark, fish-eating water birds, including the shags.
    • 🦀CrabArmoured crustaceans with the tail tucked under the body, able to run sideways.
    • 🦞CrayfishFreshwater relatives of lobsters, also called crawfish or crawdads.
    • 🐊CrocodileBig, armoured reptiles that ambush prey at the water’s edge across the tropics, from the 1.5 m dwarf crocodile to the saltwater crocodile, which can pass 6 m.
    • 🐬DolphinAround 40 species of toothed whale, from the 1.7 m Maui’s dolphin to the orca, the largest at 9.5 m.
    • 🪰DragonflyAbout 3,000 species of fast, agile predatory insects with huge compound eyes and two pairs of strong wings.
    • 🦆DuckWaterbirds of the same family as geese and swans, but generally smaller and shorter-necked.
    • 🐍EelAbout 1,000 species of long, snake-like fish, mostly marine.
    • 🐾Electric eelNocturnal freshwater fish of South America that stun prey with electric shocks.
    • 🐸FrogTailless, short-bodied amphibians, making up about 88% of all amphibian species.
    • 🐾GharialA crocodilian of the rivers of the northern Indian subcontinent, and among the longest of all, with a very long, narrow snout and 110 interlocking teeth built for catching fish.
    • 🐠GoldfishA small freshwater carp native to China, first bred for colour there over 1,000 years ago and now one of the most popular aquarium fish.
    • 🪿GooseLarge waterfowl, between ducks and swans in size.
    • 🐍Grass snakeA non-venomous, semi-aquatic Eurasian snake, usually dark green or brown with a yellow or white collar behind the head.
    • 🐠GuppyA small freshwater fish from north-east South America that gives birth to live young.
    • 🐾HellbenderA giant aquatic salamander of streams in the eastern and central United States, and the largest amphibian in North America.
    • 🐾HeronAbout 75 species of long-legged, long-necked wading birds of fresh water and coasts, including the egrets and bitterns.
    • 🦛HippopotamusA huge, semi-aquatic grazer that spends its days in African rivers and lakes and comes out at night to feed.
    • 🐾Kingfisher118 species of small to medium, brightly coloured birds with large heads and long, sharp bills.
    • 🐾LeechSegmented worms related to earthworms, with a sucker at each end.
    • 🐾LungfishSix species of air-breathing freshwater fish in Africa, South America and Australia, the closest living relatives of land vertebrates.
    • 🦆MallardA dabbling duck that breeds across the Americas, Eurasia and North Africa and has been introduced to Australia, New Zealand, South America and South Africa.
    • 🐾ManateeThree species of large, slow, fully aquatic plant-eating mammals, sometimes called sea cows, with paddle-shaped tails.
    • 🐾MayflyOver 3,000 species of ancient insects with upright wings and long tails.
    • 🐾MinkThe American mink, a semiaquatic mustelid native to North America.
    • 🫎MooseThe tallest, largest and heaviest living deer, known as “elk” in British English.
    • 🦟MosquitoAbout 3,600 species of small flies.
    • 🐾MuskratA large, semi-aquatic rodent of North American wetlands with thick brown fur and a scaly, flattened tail used as a rudder.
    • 🐾MusselBivalve molluscs of salt and fresh water with long, uneven shells, often dark blue, black or brown.
    • 🦎NewtOver 100 species of semiaquatic salamanders in North America, Europe, North Africa and Asia.
    • 🐾OlmA blind, pale salamander that lives its whole life underwater in the flooded limestone caves of the Dinaric Alps, the only cave-only vertebrate genus in Europe.
    • 🦅OspreyA large, fish-eating bird of prey, brown above and white below, found near water on every continent except Antarctica.
    • 🦦OtterSemiaquatic members of the weasel family, with long slim bodies, webbed feet and very dense fur that keeps them warm and buoyant.
    • 🐾PelicanEight species of large water birds with a long beak and a huge throat pouch, used to scoop up fish and drain out the water before swallowing.
    • 🐟PiranhaFreshwater fish of South American rivers and floodplains with razor-sharp teeth.
    • 🦆PlatypusA duck-billed, egg-laying mammal of eastern Australia and Tasmania.
    • 🐡PufferfishFish that puff up into a ball when threatened.
    • 🦎SalamanderTailed amphibians with slender, lizard-like bodies and moist skin, which keeps them near water or damp places.
    • 🐟SalmonFish that hatch in rivers, go to sea to grow, then return to fresh water to spawn, usually in the stream where they hatched.
    • 🐾SawfishVery large rays with a long, flat snout lined with teeth like a saw.
    • 🐾Sea spongeSimple animals fixed to the seabed, with bodies full of pores and channels.
    • 🐦SeagullSeabirds, usually grey and white, with strong bills and webbed feet.
    • 🦐ShrimpCrustaceans with long bodies, long antennae and thin legs, which swim forward with paddles under the tail and flick backwards to escape.
    • 🐌SnailA gastropod with a coiled shell big enough to pull itself into.
    • 🐾StingrayAbout 220 species of flat, cartilaginous fish with a venomous barbed stinger on the tail.
    • 🐟SturgeonThe Atlantic sturgeon, a big bottom-feeding fish armoured with five rows of bony plates instead of true scales.
    • 🦢SwanThe largest waterfowl, and among the largest flying birds in their range, with six living species.
    • 🐾TardigradeAbout 1,500 species of plump, eight-legged micro-animals, usually about 0.5 mm long, also called water bears or moss piglets.
    • 🐟TroutFreshwater fish closely related to salmon.
    • 🐢TurtleShelled reptiles, about 360 species, including land-living tortoises, freshwater terrapins and sea turtles.
    • 🐠ZebrafishA small striped minnow from fresh water in South Asia, sold as the zebra danio and widely used in lab research.
