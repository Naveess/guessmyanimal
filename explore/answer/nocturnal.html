    Nocturnal animals

    Animals that are mostly active at night. Following how most people play, this includes animals active at dawn and dusk. Animals that keep no strong schedule are answered "Day & night" and aren't listed here.

    Some are surprising: lions, hippos and domestic cats are all on it.

    131 animals, A to Z.

    • 🐾AardvarkA burrowing, nocturnal African mammal that digs out ants and termites with powerful claws and collects them with a long, sticky tongue.
    • 🐊AlligatorTwo species of broad-snouted crocodilian: the American alligator of the south-eastern US, and the much smaller Chinese alligator, which is critically endangered.
    • 🐍AnacondaLarge, semiaquatic boas of tropical South America.
    • 🦫ArmadilloTwenty-one species of American mammals with a leathery, armoured shell and long digging claws.
    • 🐾Aye-ayeA lemur of Madagascar and the world’s largest nocturnal primate, with rodent-like teeth that never stop growing and a thin, bony middle finger.
    • 🦡BadgerStocky, short-legged omnivores of the weasel and skunk families.
    • 🐾BandicootMore than 20 species of small, mostly nocturnal marsupials from Australia and New Guinea, with long pointed snouts, large ears and strong hind legs.
    • 🦇BatThe only mammals capable of true, sustained flight, on wings of skin stretched over long fingers.
    • 🦫BeaverLarge semiaquatic rodents, the second-largest living rodents after capybaras.
    • 🐾BilbyThe greater bilby: a long-eared, rabbit-sized marsupial of Australia that lives in burrows and comes out at night.
    • 🐾BinturongA tree-dwelling viverrid of South and Southeast Asia, also called the bearcat, with long, thick, mostly dark hair.
    • 🐆Black pantherNot a species: a black panther is a black (melanistic) leopard or jaguar.
    • 🕷️Black widowThe widow spiders: 34 species of small spiders with unusually potent venom.
    • 🐍Boa constrictorA large, heavy-bodied, non-venomous snake of tropical America, and a staple of pet collections.
    • 🐆BobcatA medium-sized North American wild cat with tufted ears and the short, "bobbed" tail that gives it its name.
    • 🐾BongoA large, mostly nocturnal forest antelope of sub-Saharan Africa with a chestnut coat, 10–15 thin white stripes and heavy spiral horns on both sexes.
    • 🐸BullfrogThe largest true frog in North America, named for the male’s call, which sounds like a bull bellowing.
    • 🐾BushbabySmall nocturnal primates of sub-Saharan Africa, with big eyes, sharp hearing, bat-like ears and long tails for balance.
    • 🐾CaecilianMore than 220 species of limbless, worm- or snake-shaped amphibians with small eyes or none, living hidden in soil or streambeds.
    • 🐊CaimanSix species of alligator relatives from Central and South America, living in marshes, swamps, lakes and mangrove rivers and fairly nocturnal.
    • 🐆CaracalA slender, long-legged wild cat with a reddish-tan coat and long black ear tufts; its name comes from the Turkish for "black ear".
    • 🐟CatfishA huge, varied order of fish named for their whisker-like barbels.
    • 🐾CentipedePredatory arthropods with one pair of legs per body segment.
    • 🐾ChinchillaTwo species of crepuscular rodents from the high Andes, living in colonies up to 4,270 m.
    • 🐾CivetOver a dozen species of small, lean, mostly nocturnal mammals of tropical Asia and Africa, most in the family Viverridae.
    • 🐆Clouded leopardA medium-sized wild cat of Asian forests, named for the large cloud-like blotches on its grey or ochre coat.
    • 🪳CockroachAbout 4,600 species of flat-bodied insects, only about 30 of which live around people.
    • 🐾CoelacanthA lobe-finned fish more closely related to lungfish and land vertebrates than to most fish.
    • 🐾CoralMost corals pull their tentacles in by day and spread them at night to catch plankton.
    • 🐈CougarA large, plain-coated cat of the Americas, also called puma, mountain lion and panther.
    • 🐺CoyoteA North American wild dog, smaller than its close relative the grey wolf.
    • 🦞CrayfishFreshwater relatives of lobsters, also called crawfish or crawdads.
    • 🦗CricketMostly nocturnal insects related to bush crickets and grasshoppers, with round heads, long antennae and powerful jumping legs.
    • 🐊CrocodileBig, armoured reptiles that ambush prey at the water’s edge across the tropics, from the 1.5 m dwarf crocodile to the saltwater crocodile, which can pass 6 m.
    • 🐱Domestic catCats are crepuscular, most active around dawn and dusk, though pet cats partly adjust to their owners’ hours.
    • 🐾DormouseAbout 28 species of small, nocturnal, tree-climbing rodents from Africa, Asia and Europe.
    • 🐾EarwigAbout 2,000 species of insects with forceps-like pincers on the tail, found on every continent except Antarctica.
    • 🐍EelAbout 1,000 species of long, snake-like fish, mostly marine.
    • 🐾Electric eelNocturnal freshwater fish of South America that stun prey with electric shocks.
    • 🦊Fennec foxThe smallest fox, from the deserts of North Africa.
    • 🦡FerretFerrets sleep 14–18 hours a day and are most active at dawn and dusk.
    • 🐾FireflyMore than 2,400 species of soft-bodied beetles, many of which glow, mostly at twilight, to attract mates.
    • 🐿️Flying squirrelAbout 50 species of squirrels that glide between trees on a furred membrane stretching from wrist to ankle, steering with their limbs and tail.
    • 🐸FrogTailless, short-bodied amphibians, making up about 88% of all amphibian species.
    • 🦇Fruit batAbout 197 species of megabats, including the flying foxes, the family with the largest bats.
    • 🦎GeckoNearly 2,000 species of small, mostly nocturnal lizards, the most species-rich lizard group.
    • 🐾GenetSeventeen species of slender, cat-like carnivores with spotted fur, a dark stripe down the spine and a ringed tail almost as long as the body.
    • 🦀Ghost crabA pale, box-bodied crab of tropical and subtropical sandy beaches worldwide, with long, thick eyestalks and one claw bigger than the other.
    • 🐾HagfishEel-shaped, jawless sea fish, usually about 50 cm long and dull pink to blue-grey, with loose skin and no true fins.
    • 🐹HamsterWild hamsters are crepuscular, active at twilight; pet hamsters become nocturnal, waking around sundown.
    • 🐇HareLong-legged relatives of rabbits that live above ground and rely on speed, not burrows, to escape.
    • 🦔HedgehogSmall, spiny insect-eaters of Europe, Asia and Africa that roll into a tight ball when threatened.
    • 🐾HellbenderA giant aquatic salamander of streams in the eastern and central United States, and the largest amphibian in North America.
    • 🦀Hermit crabAbout 800 species of crustaceans that protect their soft abdomen in empty snail shells.
    • 🦛HippopotamusStays in water or mud to keep cool by day, then comes out at dusk to graze.
    • 🐾Horseshoe crabNot a crab at all but a relative of spiders and scorpions, little changed for about 250 million years.
    • 🐺HyenaFour species of dog-like carnivores, including the spotted, brown and striped hyenas.
    • 🐺JackalJackals are crepuscular: most active at dawn and dusk.
    • 🐾JerboaSmall, nocturnal hopping rodents of the hot deserts of North Africa and Asia, with very long back legs, a tuft-tipped tail longer than the body, and often huge ears.
    • 🐾KakapoA large, nocturnal, ground-dwelling parrot found only in New Zealand, with mottled moss-green feathers and an owl-like face.
    • 🦘KangarooMany kangaroos are nocturnal or crepuscular, resting in the shade on hot days and feeding in the cool of evening, night and early morning.
    • 🐾KatydidMore than 8,000 species of long-horned relatives of grasshoppers, also called bush crickets.
    • 🐾KinkajouA tree-dwelling relative of raccoons and coatis from the rainforests of Mexico and Central and South America.
    • 🐾KiwiFive species of flightless bird found only in New Zealand, about the size of a chicken and the smallest ratites.
    • 🐨KoalaA tree-dwelling marsupial of eastern and southern Australia, and not a bear.
    • 🐆LeopardThe most widespread wild cat, a spotted, secretive hunter that survives almost anywhere with cover: rainforest, savanna, mountains and semi-desert across Africa and Asia.
    • 🦁LionMostly active at night and around dawn and dusk; lions spend much of the hot day resting.
    • 🐠LionfishVenomous reef fish of the Indo-Pacific with bold red, brown or black and white stripes and long, fan-like fins tipped with venomous spines.
    • 🦞LobsterLarge marine crustaceans with long bodies, muscular tails and big front claws, highly prized as seafood.
    • 🐆LynxFour species of medium-sized wild cat, the Canada, Eurasian and Iberian lynx and the bobcat, with black ear tufts, a short tail and big padded paws for walking on snow.
    • 🐾MillipedeAbout 12,000 species of slow-moving arthropods with two pairs of legs on most body segments.
    • 🐾MinkThe American mink, a semiaquatic mustelid native to North America.
    • 🐾Moray eelAbout 200 species of eels found worldwide, almost all marine.
    • 🦟MosquitoAbout 3,600 species of small flies.
    • 🦋MothThe vast majority of the insect order that also includes butterflies, with about 160,000 species.
    • 🐭MouseMostly crepuscular or nocturnal; mice avoid bright light.
    • 🐾MuskratA large, semi-aquatic rodent of North American wetlands with thick brown fur and a scaly, flattened tail used as a rudder.
    • 🐾NautilusNine species of shelled cephalopods from the Indo-Pacific, the last living members of an ancient group.
    • 🦎NewtOver 100 species of semiaquatic salamanders in North America, Europe, North Africa and Asia.
    • 🐆OcelotA medium-sized spotted wild cat of the Americas and the largest member of its genus.
    • 🐙OctopusEight-armed molluscs, about 300 species, with three hearts and copper-based blood.
    • 🐀OpossumThe largest group of marsupials in the Americas, 126 species.
    • 🦉OwlMost owls hunt at night, but a few, like the northern hawk-owl, are active by day.
    • 🦔PangolinScale-covered, ant-eating mammals of Asia and sub-Saharan Africa, also called scaly anteaters.
    • 🐾Pine martenA brown-furred mustelid with a cream to yellow bib on its throat, found across most of Europe.
    • 🦆PlatypusMainly nocturnal, but also active at dusk in summer and during the day in winter.
    • 🐾PolecatThe European polecat: a dark brown mustelid with a pale belly and a dark mask across its face.
    • 🦔PorcupineLarge rodents covered in sharp quills.
    • 🐍PythonA family of 39 non-venomous snakes of Africa, Asia and Australia, including some of the largest snakes in the world.
    • 🐾QuokkaA cat-sized relative of kangaroos and wallabies from southwestern Australia.
    • 🐾QuollSix species of spotted carnivorous marsupials from Australia and New Guinea, also once called native cats.
    • 🦝RaccoonA North American omnivore with dexterous front paws, a black face mask and a ringed tail.
    • 🐾Raccoon dogA heavy-set, fox-like wild dog of East Asia, named for its raccoon-like face.
    • 🐀RatMedium-sized rodents, the best known being the brown and black rats that live alongside people.
    • 🐍RattlesnakeThirty-six species of venomous pit vipers of the Americas, named for the rattle of interlocking keratin segments at the tail tip.
    • 🦊Red foxMostly active at dusk and at night, though often seen by day where it isn’t disturbed.
    • 🐾Red pandaRed pandas appear to be both nocturnal and crepuscular, active at night and around dawn and dusk.
    • 🦎SalamanderTailed amphibians with slender, lizard-like bodies and moist skin, which keeps them near water or damp places.
    • 🐈Sand catA small, pale sandy cat of sandy and stony deserts far from water.
    • 🐾SawfishVery large rays with a long, flat snout lined with teeth like a saw.
    • 🦂ScorpionArachnids with eight legs, grasping pincers and a curved tail ending in a venomous sting.
    • 🐾Sea urchinAbout 950 species of spiny, globe-shaped echinoderms living on the seabed of every ocean.
    • 🦐ShrimpCrustaceans with long bodies, long antennae and thin legs, which swim forward with paddles under the tail and flick backwards to escape.
    • 🦨SkunkSkunks are crepuscular: most active at dusk and dawn.
    • 🐻Sloth bearA shaggy bear of the Indian subcontinent with a mane around its face, a long lower lip and long, sickle-shaped claws.
    • 🐾Slow lorisSlow, quiet nocturnal primates of Southeast Asia with big reflective eyes and stubby tails hidden in their fur.
    • 🐾SlugLand gastropods with no shell, or only a tiny internal one.
    • 🐌SnailA gastropod with a coiled shell big enough to pull itself into.
    • 🐆Snow leopardA big cat of the high mountains of Central and South Asia, with thick whitish-grey fur and a long, bushy tail.
    • 🕷️SpiderEight-legged arachnids with fangs that inject venom and spinnerets that make silk.
    • 🦑SquidFast-swimming molluscs with large eyes, eight arms and two long tentacles.
    • 🐾Stick insectPlant-eating insects camouflaged as sticks or leaves, found on every continent except Antarctica, most of all in the tropics.
    • 🐾Sugar gliderA small, nocturnal gliding possum named for its taste for sugary sap and nectar.
    • 🐟SwordfishA large, migratory predatory fish with a long, flat, pointed bill.
    • 🐾TapirLarge, pig-shaped plant-eaters with a short, flexible trunk.
    • 🕷️TarantulaLarge, often hairy spiders, over a thousand species in almost 200 genera.
    • 🐾TarsierTiny primates of the islands of Southeast Asia with enormous eyes: each eyeball is about 16 mm across and as big as the whole brain.
    • 🐾Tasmanian devilA dog-sized carnivorous marsupial, now found only on Tasmania, and the largest carnivorous marsupial since the thylacine died out in 1936.
    • 🐾TenrecA family of 31 species found only on Madagascar.
    • 🐯TigerMostly active from dusk into the night.
    • 🐸ToadA common name for frogs with short legs and dry, warty skin, especially the true toads.
    • 🐸Tree frogAny frog that spends most of its life in trees.
    • 🐾TuataraA lizard-like reptile found only in New Zealand, and the last survivor of the order Rhynchocephalia, the dominant group of small reptiles in the Jurassic.
    • 🦇Vampire batThree species of bats from Central and South America, the only mammals that feed only on blood.
    • 🦘WallabySmall and medium-sized relatives of the kangaroo.
    • 🐗Wild boarThe wild pig of Eurasia and North Africa, now one of the widest-ranging mammals in the world after introductions to the Americas and Oceania.
    • 🐈WildcatThe European wildcat: bigger and stouter than a house cat, with longer striped fur, a dark stripe along the back and a bushy, black-tipped tail.
    • 🐺WolfThe largest wild member of the dog family.
    • 🐨WombatMainly crepuscular and nocturnal, though wombats come out on cool or overcast days too.
    • 🐾WoodlouseLand-living crustaceans with flat, segmented bodies and seven pairs of legs.
    • 🪱WormEarthworms are soil-dwelling annelids, among nature’s most important recyclers of dead plant matter.
