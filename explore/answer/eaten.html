    Animals people eat

    "Yes" means widely eaten, the kind of thing on a normal menu somewhere. "In places" means eaten in some countries or cultures, now or traditionally, but not widely, and the note says where.

    This is a factual answer about the world, not a recommendation. Some animals marked "in places" are protected, and eating them is illegal in most of the world.

    211 animals, A to Z.

    Eaten: yes 84

    • 🐾AgoutiRodents related to guinea pigs but larger and longer-legged, living in forests and wooded areas of Central and South America.
    • 🦙AlpacaA domesticated South American camelid bred for its highly valued fibre, descended from the wild vicuña at least 5,000–6,000 years ago.
    • 🐟AnchovyEaten salted, canned or fried, and used in sauces; Peru's anchovy fishery is one of the largest in the world.
    • 🐾AntelopeNot a single group but 91 species of horned, hoofed bovids, most of them native to Africa.
    • 🐟BarracudaAbout 26 species of long, silvery predatory fish with fang-like teeth and an underbite, found in tropical and subtropical oceans worldwide, near the surface, coral reefs and seagrass.
    • 🐟BassThe largemouth bass, an olive-green freshwater fish with a dark, jagged stripe along each side, native to the eastern and central US, south-eastern Canada and northern Mexico.
    • 🦬BisonTwo species of huge, shaggy wild cattle: the American bison, often called buffalo, and the European bison, or wisent.
    • 🐃BuffaloA large, heavily built wild cow of sub-Saharan Africa.
    • 🐂BullAn adult male of cattle that has not been castrated.
    • 🐸BullfrogCommonly eaten across its range, especially in the southern US; people eat the legs.
    • 🐫CamelThree living species of humped desert animals: the one-humped dromedary (94% of the world’s camels), the two-humped Bactrian camel, and the endangered wild Bactrian camel.
    • 🐟CarpThe common carp is a large freshwater fish of lakes and rivers in Europe and Asia.
    • 🐟CatfishA huge, varied order of fish named for their whisker-like barbels.
    • 🐔ChickenThe domesticated form of the red junglefowl of Southeast Asia, first tamed around 8,000 years ago.
    • 🐟CodThe Atlantic cod is a large, brown or green spotted fish of the North Atlantic, one of the most widely eaten fish in the world.
    • 🐮CowLarge domesticated grazers, first tamed from the wild aurochs about 10,500 years ago.
    • 🦀CrabArmoured crustaceans with the tail tucked under the body, able to run sideways.
    • 🦞CrayfishFreshwater relatives of lobsters, also called crawfish or crawdads.
    • 🐾CuttlefishMarine cephalopods with W-shaped pupils, eight arms and two tentacles, and an internal shell, the cuttlebone, that controls buoyancy.
    • 🦌DeerHoofed plant-eaters whose males, in almost every species, grow and shed a new set of bony antlers every year.
    • 🐪DromedaryThe one-humped camel, and the tallest of the three camel species.
    • 🦆DuckWaterbirds of the same family as geese and swans, but generally smaller and shorter-necked.
    • 🐍EelFreshwater eels (unagi) and sea eels are popular in Japanese and Chinese cooking.
    • 🐾ElandHunted and farmed for its meat; some southern African farmers have switched from cattle to eland because it suits the climate better.
    • 🦌ElkThe second-largest deer after the moose, native to North America and Central and East Asia.
    • 🐾EmuEmus are farmed for meat, oil and leather in Australia, North America, Peru and China. Commercial farming began around 1970.
    • 🐸FrogFrogs, especially their muscular hind legs, are eaten in many parts of the world.
    • 🐾GazelleSmall, fast antelopes, mostly fawn-coloured, living in herds in the deserts, grasslands and savannas of Africa and in south-west and central Asia and India.
    • 🐐GoatOne of the first animals ever domesticated, from the wild goat in Iran about 10,000 years ago.
    • 🪿GooseLarge waterfowl, between ducks and swans in size.
    • 🦗GrasshopperGrasshoppers are eaten in countries such as Mexico and Indonesia.
    • 🐾GrouseCompact, heavily built game birds of the Northern Hemisphere, from arctic tundra to pine forest, moorland and mountains.
    • 🐹Guinea pigGuinea pigs, called cuy, are still raised for meat in the Andes of South America.
    • 🐟HalibutHighly regarded as a food fish, but the Atlantic halibut is so overfished that Seafood Watch advises avoiding it.
    • 🐇HareLong-legged relatives of rabbits that live above ground and rely on speed, not burrows, to escape.
    • 🐾HartebeestA popular game animal because its meat is highly regarded.
    • 🐟HerringOily forage fish that travel in huge schools through the shallow, temperate waters of the North Atlantic and North Pacific, including the Baltic Sea.
    • 🐾ImpalaA medium-sized antelope of eastern and southern Africa with a glossy reddish-brown coat.
    • 🦘KangarooWild kangaroos are shot for meat, which is valued for being low in fat, and they have always been an important food for Aboriginal Australians.
    • 🐾KuduHunted for its meat, which is lean and similar to venison.
    • 🐠LionfishEdible if prepared correctly; divers in Florida and Texas are encouraged to catch them for food to fight the invasion.
    • 🦙LlamaA domesticated South American camelid, used by Andean cultures for meat and as a pack animal since before Columbus.
    • 🦞LobsterLarge marine crustaceans with long bodies, muscular tails and big front claws, highly prized as seafood.
    • 🦗LocustEaten throughout history and considered a delicacy in many countries.
    • 🦆MallardA dabbling duck that breeds across the Americas, Eurasia and North Africa and has been introduced to Australia, New Zealand, South America and South Africa.
    • 🐟MarlinLarge ocean predators with a spear-like bill and a long, rigid dorsal fin.
    • 🫎MooseThe tallest, largest and heaviest living deer, known as “elk” in British English.
    • 🦌MuntjacIts low-fat meat is noted for its culinary qualities.
    • 🐾MusselBivalve molluscs of salt and fresh water with long, uneven shells, often dark blue, black or brown.
    • 🐾NyalaHunted as game; big-horned males are prized by sport hunters.
    • 🐙OctopusEight-armed molluscs, about 300 species, with three hearts and copper-based blood.
    • 🐾OryxFour species of large, pale antelopes with dark markings on the face and legs and long, nearly straight horns.
    • 🦤OstrichOstriches are farmed around the world for their meat, feathers and leather.
    • 🐾OysterSaltwater bivalve molluscs with rough, often irregular shells.
    • 🐾PartridgeMedium-sized, ground-dwelling game birds, between quail and pheasants in size, mostly grey and brown.
    • 🐾PheasantPheasants are a popular game bird, introduced to many countries to be shot by hunters.
    • 🐷PigPork is the main reason pigs are kept, but eating it is forbidden in Islam and Judaism.
    • 🐟PiranhaFreshwater fish of South American rivers and floodplains with razor-sharp teeth.
    • 🐾PronghornOnce a principal food of Plains peoples, and still legally hunted for food in the western US.
    • 🐾QuailMid-sized game birds of two groups: Old World quail, related to pheasants, and New World quail in a family of their own.
    • 🐰RabbitEvery pet and farmed rabbit belongs to one species, the European rabbit.
    • 🦌ReindeerA deer of the Arctic and subarctic, called caribou in North America.
    • 🐾RheaLarge flightless birds of South American grasslands, just behind the emu in size.
    • 🐟SailfishA hard-fighting game fish, but less valued than marlin and usually released.
    • 🐟SalmonFish that hatch in rivers, go to sea to grow, then return to fresh water to spawn, usually in the stream where they hatched.
    • 🐾ScallopHighly prized as seafood, and some species are farmed; the part usually sold as "scallop" is the big adductor muscle.
    • 🐾Sea cucumberA delicacy in Asian cuisines, sold as trepang, namako or bêche-de-mer; some species are farmed.
    • 🐾Sea urchinAbout 950 species of spiny, globe-shaped echinoderms living on the seabed of every ocean.
    • 🐑SheepDomesticated ruminants kept for wool, meat and milk, most likely descended from the wild mouflon.
    • 🦐ShrimpCrustaceans with long bodies, long antennae and thin legs, which swim forward with paddles under the tail and flick backwards to escape.
    • 🐾SilkwormThe pupae are eaten as a snack or dish in Korea (beondegi), China, Japan, Vietnam, Thailand and Assam in India.
    • 🐌SnailA gastropod with a coiled shell big enough to pull itself into.
    • 🐾SpringbokHunted as game and farmed; springbok meat is sold in South African supermarkets and exported.
    • 🦑SquidSquid are caught for food by commercial fisheries in Japan, the Mediterranean, the Atlantic, the Pacific and elsewhere.
    • 🐟SturgeonFished above all for caviar made from its eggs, and for its flesh; 19th-century overharvesting caused its collapse.
    • 🐟SwordfishA large, migratory predatory fish with a long, flat, pointed bill.
    • 🐟TroutFreshwater fish closely related to salmon.
    • 🐟TunaFifteen species of fast, streamlined ocean predators in the mackerel family.
    • 🦃TurkeyA large North American game bird, the heaviest of the chicken-like birds.
    • 🐗WarthogA wild pig of the grasslands and savannas of sub-Saharan Africa, with two pairs of upward-curving tusks.
    • 🐗Wild boarThe wild pig of Eurasia and North Africa, now one of the widest-ranging mammals in the world after introductions to the Americas and Oceania.
    • 🐾WildebeestTwo species of antelope from eastern and southern Africa, also called gnus: the blue wildebeest and the black wildebeest.
    • 🐂YakLong-haired domesticated cattle of the Himalayas, the Tibetan Plateau and the mountains of Central Asia, north to Mongolia and Siberia.
    • 🐂ZebuRaised for beef across the tropics; zebu breeds make up most of Brazil's beef cattle.

    • 🐏Bighorn sheepA wild sheep of western North America named for its large horns.

    • 🐾BongoHunted for its hide, horns and meat, and a common local source of bushmeat.
    • 🐻Brown bearA huge, mostly plant-eating bear of northern forests, mountains and tundra, and one of the largest land carnivores alive.
    • 🐊CaimanHumans are their main predator, hunting caimans for their meat and skin.
    • 🐾CapybaraCapybaras are hunted for their meat, hide and fat, and farmed in some areas.
    • 🐛CaterpillarThe larval stage of butterflies and moths, built for eating and growing.
    • 🐾CentipedePredatory arthropods with one pair of legs per body segment.
    • 🐾ChamoisA popular game animal hunted for its meat.
    • 🐾CicadaEaten in various parts of the world, including China, Myanmar, Malaysia, central Africa and parts of Mexico.
    • 🐾CivetOver a dozen species of small, lean, mostly nocturnal mammals of tropical Asia and Africa, most in the family Viverridae.
    • 🐾CoatiLong-nosed members of the raccoon family from the Americas.
    • 🐍CobraKing cobras are poached and sold as bushmeat, turned into snake leather and used in traditional Chinese medicine.
    • 🪳CockroachAbout 4,600 species of flat-bodied insects, only about 30 of which live around people.
    • 🦗CricketEaten in Southeast Asia, where they are sold deep-fried in markets as snacks.
    • 🐊CrocodileBig, armoured reptiles that ambush prey at the water’s edge across the tropics, from the 1.5 m dwarf crocodile to the saltwater crocodile, which can pass 6 m.
    • 🐾Dik-dikHunted locally, mostly with snares, for its hide and bones.
    • 🐾DodoAn extinct flightless bird of Mauritius, in the Indian Ocean, in the pigeon and dove family; its closest living relative is the Nicobar pigeon.
    • 🐶DogEating dog is taboo in most of the world, but dog meat is still eaten in some East Asian countries, including China, Vietnam and Korea.
    • 🐬DolphinDolphin meat is eaten in a few countries, including Japan and Peru.
    • 🫏DonkeyA few donkeys are raised for meat or milk; about 3.5 million donkeys and mules are slaughtered for meat each year.
    • 🐾DormouseAbout 28 species of small, nocturnal, tree-climbing rodents from Africa, Asia and Europe.
    • 🐾DugongA seagrass-eating marine mammal of the Indo-West Pacific, the manatee’s relative and the only living member of its family.
    • 🐾EchidnaFour species of spiny, egg-laying mammals from Australia and New Guinea, also called spiny anteaters.
    • 🦇Fruit batAbout 197 species of megabats, including the flying foxes, the family with the largest bats.
    • 🦀Ghost crabA pale, box-bodied crab of tropical and subtropical sandy beaches worldwide, with long, thick eyestalks and one claw bigger than the other.
    • 🦧GibbonThe lesser apes: about 20 species of tailless apes of Asian forests.
    • 🐻Grizzly bearThe North American brown bear, a population or subspecies of the brown bear.
    • 🐾GroundhogA large ground squirrel, also called the woodchuck, and the only lowland marmot.
    • 🐾GuanacoA wild camelid of South America and the ancestor of the domestic llama.
    • 🐾HagfishRarely eaten in most of the world, but valued in South Korea, where it is grilled or stir-fried; one species is also eaten in Japan.
    • 🦔HedgehogEaten in ancient Egypt and medieval Europe, and still eaten by some Romani communities.
    • 🐝HornetIn some Japanese mountain villages, Asian giant hornet larvae are a fried delicacy.
    • 🐴HorseHorse meat is eaten in many parts of the world. About 5 million horses are slaughtered for meat each year.
    • 🐾Horseshoe crabEaten as a delicacy in parts of Asia; in the United States it is caught for fishing bait and for its blood.
    • 🐒Howler monkeyFifteen species of large New World monkeys, the most widespread primate genus in the Neotropics.
    • 🐋Humpback whaleA baleen whale with very long flippers and knobbly bumps on its head, famous for breaching and for the long, complex songs males sing.
    • 🐾IbexSeveral species of wild goat whose males carry large curved horns, ridged across the front.
    • 🦎IguanaIguana meat is traditional in Mexico and Central America and also eaten in the Dominican Republic and Puerto Rico. The eggs are eaten in places too.
    • 🐾IndriMany Malagasy taboos (fady) protect it, but it is still hunted and in some regions its meat is prized.
    • 🪼JellyfishSome jellyfish are eaten, pressed and salted, as a delicacy in some Asian countries.
    • 🐾KatydidSome species are eaten, such as the nsenene of Uganda and neighbouring areas.
    • 🐾KrillSmall, shrimp-like marine crustaceans found in every ocean, near the bottom of the food chain.
    • 🦧LemurRing-tailed lemurs are hunted locally for bushmeat, one reason they’re endangered.
    • 🐾LimpetEaten in Portugal (lapas) and Asturias in Spain; in Ireland it was famine food, so tough it had to be boiled or roasted thoroughly.
    • 🦎LizardGreen iguanas are eaten in Central America, and spiny-tailed lizards in Africa.
    • 🐾LungfishSix species of air-breathing freshwater fish in Africa, South America and Australia, the closest living relatives of land vertebrates.
    • 🐒MacaqueA genus of 23 species of sturdy, social monkeys.
    • 🐾ManateeThree species of large, slow, fully aquatic plant-eating mammals, sometimes called sea cows, with paddle-shaped tails.
    • 🐒MandrillA large monkey of west-central African rainforests and one of the most colourful mammals, with red and blue skin on its face and rump.
    • 🐾Manta rayThree species of huge rays of warm seas, with triangular fins and horn-shaped flaps by the mouth.
    • 🐾Mantis shrimpEaten in Japan (as shako sushi), Vietnam, southern China, the Philippines, Kiribati and around the Mediterranean.
    • 🐾MarkhorPoached for food and for its horns, which some believe have medicinal properties.
    • 🐾MarmotFifteen species of large ground squirrels, the heaviest members of the squirrel family, from Asia, Europe and North America.
    • 🐾MayflyEaten in several cultures: pressed into kungu cakes in Malawi, collected around Lake Victoria, and eaten in parts of China and Japan.
    • 🦎Monitor lizardSome peoples in India, Nepal, the Philippines, Australia and Africa eat their meat, and in Vietnam and Thailand it is a delicacy.
    • 🐵MonkeyMonkeys are eaten as bushmeat in parts of Africa, and monkey brains are eaten as a delicacy in parts of South Asia, Africa and China.
    • 🐾Moray eelAbout 200 species of eels found worldwide, almost all marine.
    • 🦋MothSome caterpillars, the larvae of moths and butterflies, are eaten by people.
    • 🐐Mountain goatA sure-footed, white-coated climber of the high mountains of western North America, often seen on near-vertical cliffs.
    • 🐾MuleA hybrid of a male donkey and a female horse, and more common than the hinny, the reverse cross.
    • 🐾MuskoxRarely farmed for meat; the muskox farms in Alaska raise it mainly for its wool.
    • 🐾MuskratEaten in parts of North America; in south-east Michigan Catholics are allowed to eat it on Lenten Fridays.
    • 🐾NarwhalA toothed whale of the Arctic, closely related to the beluga.
    • 🐀OpossumThe largest group of marsupials in the Americas, 126 species.
    • 🦔PangolinPangolins are poached for their meat and for their scales, which are used in traditional medicine.
    • 🐠ParrotfishLarger species are fished commercially, mainly in the Indo-Pacific, but most species are not heavily fished.
    • 🦚PeacockPeafowl are three species of pheasant: the Indian and green peafowl of Asia and the Congo peafowl of Central Africa.
    • 🐦PigeonThe familiar city pigeon is the domestic form of the wild rock dove, native to southern Europe, North Africa and western Asia.
    • 🐴PonyA type of small horse, with a thicker coat, mane and tail, shorter legs, heavier bones and a wider body than larger horses.
    • 🦔PorcupineIn Kenya, porcupines have become crop pests and are eaten as a delicacy.
    • 🐾PorpoiseEight species of small, dolphin-like toothed whales, more closely related to narwhals and belugas than to dolphins.
    • 🐡PufferfishIt is a delicacy in Japan (fugu), Korea and China, prepared only by specially trained chefs.
    • 🐾PuffinThe Atlantic puffin is a small seabird of the auk family with a big red-and-black beak.
    • 🐍PythonPythons are sold and eaten as meat, and poached for their skins.
    • 🦝RaccoonA North American omnivore with dexterous front paws, a black face mask and a ringed tail.
    • 🐾Raccoon dogA heavy-set, fox-like wild dog of East Asia, named for its raccoon-like face.
    • 🐀RatRat meat is taboo in some cultures but a dietary staple in others.
    • 🐍RattlesnakeThirty-six species of venomous pit vipers of the Americas, named for the rattle of interlocking keratin segments at the tail tip.
    • 🐾SaigaHunted for meat, which is compared to lamb; hunting reopened in Kazakhstan in 2025.
    • 🐾SawfishHunted for its fins, used in shark fin soup, and for its teeth and saw in traditional medicine.
    • 🦂ScorpionArachnids with eight legs, grasping pincers and a curved tail ending in a venomous sting.
    • 🐾Sea anemoneEaten in a few places, such as a curry soup in parts of Thailand and in Zhejiang, China.
    • 🦭Sea lionEared seals with external ear flaps, long front flippers and the ability to walk on all fours.
    • 🐢Sea turtleSeven species of ocean-going turtles, including the green, loggerhead, hawksbill and leatherback.
    • 🐠SeahorseDried seahorses are widely used in traditional Chinese medicine, despite no clinical evidence that they work.
    • 🦭SealArctic Indigenous peoples have traditionally used the meat, blubber and skin of seals.
    • 🦈SharkSharks are caught for their meat and their fins.
    • 🦥SlothSlow-moving tree mammals of Central and South American rainforests, named for their very low metabolism.
    • 🐾SlugLand gastropods with no shell, or only a tiny internal one.
    • 🐍SnakeLimbless reptiles with more than 4,170 species, found on every continent except Antarctica.
    • 🐋Sperm whaleThe largest toothed whale and the largest toothed predator.
    • 🕷️SpiderEight-legged arachnids with fangs that inject venom and spinnerets that make silk.
    • 🐒Spider monkeySeven species of long-limbed New World monkeys that live high in the rainforest canopy.
    • 🐿️SquirrelRodents with slender bodies, long bushy tails and large eyes.
    • 🐾StarfishStar-shaped echinoderms, not fish, with about 1,900 species on the seabed of every ocean.
    • 🐾StingrayAbout 220 species of flat, cartilaginous fish with a venomous barbed stinger on the tail.
    • 🐾StonefishVenomous reef fish of the coastal Indo-Pacific, grey and mottled so they look like a stone or a lump of coral.
    • 🐾TapirLarge, pig-shaped plant-eaters with a short, flexible trunk.
    • 🕷️TarantulaTarantulas are eaten as a delicacy in places such as Venezuela and Cambodia, roasted over a fire to remove the bristles.
    • 🐾TenrecEaten by some people on Mauritius and Mayotte, where the tailless tenrec was introduced.
    • 🐾TermiteEaten in some cultures; winged alates are high in fat and protein, and queens are regarded as a delicacy.
    • 🐢TerrapinThe diamondback was a gourmet delicacy in the early 1900s and was hunted almost to extinction.
    • 🐢TortoiseLand-living turtles with domed shells and column-like, elephant-like legs.
    • 🐢TurtleShelled reptiles, about 360 species, including land-living tortoises, freshwater terrapins and sea turtles.
    • 🐒Vervet monkeyAn African monkey with a black face and grey body hair, living in groups of 10 to 70.
    • 🦘WallabySmall and medium-sized relatives of the kangaroo.
    • 🦭WalrusIndigenous Arctic peoples have long hunted walruses for meat, fat, skin, tusks and bone.
    • 🐳WhaleArctic Indigenous peoples have traditionally eaten whale meat and blubber.
    • 🐨WombatShort-legged, muscular marsupials of Australia that dig large burrows with rodent-like front teeth and powerful claws.
    • 🪱WormEarthworms are soil-dwelling annelids, among nature’s most important recyclers of dead plant matter.
    • 🐠WrasseOver 600 species of often brightly coloured sea fish with thick lips.
