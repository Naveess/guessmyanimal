    Dangerous animals

    The site asks "could it seriously hurt a person?", not "is it fierce?". "Yes" means it's known to seriously injure or kill people: big predators, heavyweights like hippos and buffalo, venomous animals, and disease carriers like mosquitoes and ticks. "Can be" means it can hurt you, but usually only if it's provoked, cornered or handled.

    The note under each animal explains its answer where one is needed. This is a guide for a game, not safety advice.

    145 animals, A to Z.

    Dangerous: yes 45

    • 🐊AlligatorAlligators can and do attack people, often when someone gets close to them or their nests.
    • 🐍AnacondaLarge, semiaquatic boas of tropical South America.
    • 🦬BisonTwo species of huge, shaggy wild cattle: the American bison, often called buffalo, and the European bison, or wisent.
    • 🐆Black pantherNot a species: a black panther is a black (melanistic) leopard or jaguar.
    • 🕷️Black widowOnly females’ bites are dangerous, and they rarely cause death; the US recorded no black widow deaths from 1983 to 2012.
    • 🐍BoomslangA highly venomous tree snake of sub-Saharan Africa; its name is Afrikaans for "tree snake".
    • 🐻Brown bearA huge, mostly plant-eating bear of northern forests, mountains and tundra, and one of the largest land carnivores alive.
    • 🐃BuffaloTrophy hunters prize it for its aggression and power.
    • 🐂BullAn adult male of cattle that has not been castrated.
    • 🐊CaimanSix species of alligator relatives from Central and South America, living in marshes, swamps, lakes and mangrove rivers and fairly nocturnal.
    • 🐾CassowaryThree species of huge, flightless birds from the tropical forests of New Guinea, nearby islands and northeastern Australia.
    • 🐍CobraVenomous snakes that rear up and spread a hood when threatened.
    • 🐍CopperheadVenomous but not aggressive; bites are rarely fatal, and many are "dry", with little or no venom.
    • 🐊CrocodileBig, armoured reptiles that ambush prey at the water’s edge across the tropics, from the 1.5 m dwarf crocodile to the saltwater crocodile, which can pass 6 m.
    • 🐾Electric eelNocturnal freshwater fish of South America that stun prey with electric shocks.
    • 🐘ElephantThree living species, the African bush elephant, the African forest elephant and the Asian elephant, and the largest land animals alive.
    • 🐻Grizzly bearThe North American brown bear, a population or subspecies of the brown bear.
    • 🦛HippopotamusAggressive and unpredictable, and counted among the most dangerous animals in the world.
    • 🧍HumanThe most abundant and widespread primate: an upright-walking ape with little body hair and a large, complex brain that allows technology, culture and language.
    • 🐺HyenaSpotted hyenas are usually shy around people, but they do occasionally prey on humans, and attacks are likely under-reported.
    • 🐆JaguarThe biggest cat in the Americas and the only one of the roaring big cats native there.
    • 🪼JellyfishStings from some box jellyfish, such as Chironex fleckeri, are extremely painful and often fatal to people.
    • 🦎Komodo dragonThe largest living lizard, a monitor lizard found only on a few Indonesian islands.
    • 🐆LeopardThe most widespread wild cat, a spotted, secretive hunter that survives almost anywhere with cover: rainforest, savanna, mountains and semi-desert across Africa and Asia.
    • 🐾LigerThe hybrid of a male lion and a female tiger, and the largest living cat.
    • 🦁LionAfrica's social big cat.
    • 🐠LionfishStings cause severe pain and can be fatal, mostly to young children, the elderly or people allergic to the venom; they are rarely fatal to healthy adults.
    • 🐍MambaFour species of fast, highly venomous African snakes in the cobra family.
    • 🦟MosquitoMosquito-borne diseases infect nearly 700 million people a year and kill over one million.
    • 🐸Poison dart frogThe golden poison frog carries enough toxin to kill ten to twenty people, but captive-bred frogs have almost none.
    • 🐻‍❄️Polar bearThe largest bear and the largest land carnivore, living on Arctic sea ice and hunting seals.
    • 🐡PufferfishEating the wrong parts is often fatal: the liver and sometimes the skin carry tetrodotoxin.
    • 🐍RattlesnakeRattlesnakes cause the most snakebites in North America, but they rarely bite unless provoked, and treated bites are seldom fatal.
    • 🦏RhinocerosFive living species of huge, thick-skinned plant-eaters, two in Africa and three in South and Southeast Asia.
    • 🦂ScorpionMost stings don’t need medical treatment, but about 25 species, under 1%, can kill a person. Deaths happen mainly where medical help is hard to reach.
    • 🦈SharkOnly four of more than 470 species, the great white, oceanic whitetip, tiger and bull sharks, are behind a significant number of fatal, unprovoked attacks.
    • 🐍SidewinderA small rattlesnake of the deserts of the southwestern US and northwestern Mexico.
    • 🐻Sloth bearA shaggy bear of the Indian subcontinent with a mane around its face, a long lower lip and long, sickle-shaped claws.
    • 🐍SnakeMost snakes aren’t venomous, but snakebites kill an estimated 100,000 people a year, according to the WHO.
    • 🐾StonefishThe most venomous fish known: stings are extremely painful and can kill if untreated. Hot water and antivenom are the main treatments.
    • 🐍TaipanThree species of large, fast, extremely venomous snakes from Australia and New Guinea, in the cobra family.
    • 🐾TickTicks spread serious diseases, such as Lyme disease and tick-borne encephalitis.
    • 🐯TigerThe biggest of the striped cats and a solitary ambush hunter of forests, from Indian mangroves to snowy Russian taiga.
    • 🐾TigonThe hybrid of a male tiger and a lioness.
    • 🐍ViperAll vipers are venomous, but the common adder is not aggressive, and its bites, though very painful, are rarely fatal.

    Dangerous: can be 100

    • 🐕African wild dogThe largest wild canine in Africa, with a patchy brown, black and white coat.
    • 🐜AnteaterFour species of toothless mammals that eat ants and termites, related to sloths: the giant anteater, the silky anteater and two tamanduas.
    • 🐾ApeThe tailless primates of sub-Saharan Africa and Southeast Asia: the gibbons, or lesser apes, and the great apes, which are the orangutans, gorillas, chimpanzees, bonobos and, scientifically, humans.
    • 🐾BabirusaWild pigs of Sulawesi and nearby Indonesian islands, also called deer-pigs.
    • 🐒BaboonSix species of large, ground-dwelling monkeys with long, dog-like muzzles and powerful jaws.
    • 🦡BadgerStocky, short-legged omnivores of the weasel and skunk families.
    • 🐟BarracudaBites on swimmers are rare and are thought to happen in poor visibility or when a barracuda mistakes shiny jewellery for prey.
    • 🦇BatBats carry diseases such as rabies, which makes handling them risky.
    • 🐝BeeFlying insects that feed on nectar and pollen, with over 20,000 species.
    • 🐻Black bearThe smallest and most widespread bear in North America, mostly living in forests.
    • 🐍Boa constrictorA large, heavy-bodied, non-venomous snake of tropical America, and a staple of pet collections.
    • 🐆BobcatA medium-sized North American wild cat with tufted ears and the short, "bobbed" tail that gives it its name.
    • 🐝BumblebeeFemales can sting repeatedly but generally ignore humans and other animals.
    • 🐫CamelThree living species of humped desert animals: the one-humped dromedary (94% of the world’s camels), the two-humped Bactrian camel, and the endangered wild Bactrian camel.
    • 🐆CaracalA slender, long-legged wild cat with a reddish-tan coat and long black ear tufts; its name comes from the Turkish for "black ear".
    • 🐛CaterpillarSome have venomous hairs: the South American Lonomia caterpillar’s venom can cause fatal bleeding in humans.
    • 🐾CentipedeAll centipedes are venomous and can inflict painful stings.
    • 🐆CheetahThe fastest land animal: a lightweight, long-legged cat built for short sprints across open ground.
    • 🐒ChimpanzeeAttacks by wild great apes are rare, but chimpanzees are the most likely to attack, mostly children, near forest edges and farmland.
    • 🐆Clouded leopardA medium-sized wild cat of Asian forests, named for the large cloud-like blotches on its grey or ochre coat.
    • 🐈CougarAttacks on people are very rare. They usually involve a young or starving cougar, or one that has got used to people.
    • 🐮CowLarge domesticated grazers, first tamed from the wild aurochs about 10,500 years ago.
    • 🐺CoyoteA North American wild dog, smaller than its close relative the grey wolf.
    • 🦌DeerHoofed plant-eaters whose males, in almost every species, grow and shed a new set of bony antlers every year.
    • 🐾DholeA wild dog of South, East and Southeast Asia and the only member of its genus.
    • 🐕DingoA lean, hardy wild dog of Australia, ginger, black-and-tan or creamy white.
    • 🐶DogMost dogs are harmless, but dog bites affect tens of millions of people a year, and children suffer the most serious injuries.
    • 🐪DromedaryGenerally non-aggressive, except for males in the rut.
    • 🦅EagleLarge birds of prey with huge hooked beaks and powerful talons.
    • 🐍EelAbout 1,000 species of long, snake-like fish, mostly marine.
    • 🦌ElkThe second-largest deer after the moose, native to North America and Central and East Asia.
    • 🐾EmuEmus have legs strong enough to tear down metal fencing and defend their young fiercely, but there are only two documented cases of emus attacking people.
    • 🦅FalconAbout 40 species of fast birds of prey with thin, tapered wings, found on every continent except Antarctica.
    • 🐾FleaFleas carry disease: the oriental rat flea spreads the bacterium that causes bubonic plague.
    • 🐾FossaThe largest mammalian carnivore on Madagascar: a slender, cat-like hunter with a tail nearly as long as its body.
    • 🐸FrogMost are harmless to touch, but frog skin secretions range from distasteful to toxic, and brightly coloured species warn predators they’re poisonous.
    • 🦒GiraffeThe tallest living land animal, browsing leaves from treetops across African savanna.
    • 🪿GooseGeese are highly aggressive and noisy when they feel threatened, and ganders stand between a threat and their family.
    • 🐾GopherUsually flees, but if cornered it may attack cats and people and can inflict serious bites.
    • 🦍GorillaThe largest living primates: great apes of the tropical forests of equatorial Africa, living in troops led by a silverback male.
    • 🦅HawkBirds of prey of the eagle family, found on every continent except Antarctica.
    • 🐝HornetSingle stings are rarely fatal except to allergic people, but multiple Asian giant hornet stings kill 30–50 people a year in Japan.
    • 🐴HorseA one-toed, hoofed grazer, domesticated on the steppes of western Eurasia.
    • 🐾HorseflyBites can hurt for a day or more, and females can spread blood-borne diseases between animals.
    • 🐺JackalWild dogs of Africa and Eurasia: the black-backed and side-striped jackals of Africa and the golden jackal of south-central Europe and Asia.
    • 🦘KangarooUnprovoked attacks on people are very rare.
    • 🐆LynxFour species of medium-sized wild cat, the Canada, Eurasian and Iberian lynx and the bobcat, with black ear tufts, a short tail and big padded paws for walking on snow.
    • 🐒MacaqueA genus of 23 species of sturdy, social monkeys.
    • 🐒MandrillA large monkey of west-central African rainforests and one of the most colourful mammals, with red and blue skin on its face and rump.
    • 🐾Mantis shrimpNicknamed "thumb splitter": it can inflict painful wounds if handled carelessly.
    • 🐟MarlinLarge ocean predators with a spear-like bill and a long, rigid dorsal fin.
    • 🦎Monitor lizardAbout 94 species of lizards with long necks, powerful tails and claws and strong legs, from 20 cm long to the 3 m Komodo dragon.
    • 🐵MonkeyPrimates of the Americas, Africa and Asia, split into New World and Old World monkeys.
    • 🫎MooseMoose aren’t usually aggressive, but when provoked or frightened they attack more people than bears and wolves combined, usually with only minor injuries.
    • 🐾Moray eelAbout 200 species of eels found worldwide, almost all marine.
    • 🐾MuskoxUsually not aggressive, but it has killed people who came too close, including in Norway in 1964 and Alaska in 2022.
    • 🦎NewtMany newts have toxic skin. The rough-skinned newt of the Pacific Northwest carries enough tetrodotoxin to kill an adult human.
    • 🐆OcelotA medium-sized spotted wild cat of the Americas and the largest member of its genus.
    • 🐙OctopusAll octopuses are venomous, but only the blue-ringed octopuses are known to be deadly to people.
    • 🦧OrangutanGreat apes that live almost entirely in trees, now found only on Borneo and Sumatra.
    • 🐋OrcaNo fatal attack by a wild orca has ever been recorded, but captive orcas have injured and killed their handlers in marine parks.
    • 🐾OryxFour species of large, pale antelopes with dark markings on the face and legs and long, nearly straight horns.
    • 🦤OstrichIf cornered, an ostrich can attack with a powerful kick.
    • 🐼PandaUsually docile, but giant pandas have attacked people on rare occasions.
    • 🐷PigA domesticated omnivore farmed mainly for pork.
    • 🐟PiranhaDespite the media image, piranhas usually aren’t a serious risk to people, though attacks do happen.
    • 🦆PlatypusMales have a spur on each hind foot that delivers venom, strong enough to cause severe pain and swelling in people.
    • 🦔PorcupineLarge rodents covered in sharp quills.
    • 🐍PythonPythons aren’t venomous, and only the reticulated python is known to sometimes eat people. Larger species need caution as pets.
    • 🦝RaccoonA North American omnivore with dexterous front paws, a black face mask and a ringed tail.
    • 🐟SailfishUsually no threat, but sailfish have attacked in self-defence: one stabbed a woman with its bill when her party tried to catch it.
    • 🦎SalamanderSome species’ skin contains the powerful poison tetrodotoxin, advertised by bright warning colours.
    • 🐾SawfishDocile and generally harmless, but a captured sawfish defending itself can cause serious injuries with its saw.
    • 🦭Sea lionEared seals with external ear flaps, long front flippers and the ability to walk on all fours.
    • 🐾Sea urchinTheir sharp spines protect them, and in some species the spines are venomous.
    • 🦭SealFlippered marine mammals: the true seals, the sea lions and fur seals, and the walrus, 34 species in all.
    • 🐆ServalA slender, medium-sized African wild cat with a small head, big ears and a golden coat spotted and striped with black.
    • 🐾Slow lorisIts bite is toxic, which is rare among mammals. It gets the toxin by licking a gland on its arm, and saliva activates it.
    • 🐆Snow leopardA big cat of the high mountains of Central and South Asia, with thick whitish-grey fur and a long, bushy tail.
    • 🐻Spectacled bearThe only bear native to South America, living in the Andes, and the last of the short-faced bears.
    • 🐋Sperm whaleThe largest toothed whale and the largest toothed predator.
    • 🕷️SpiderMost bites are no worse than a bee sting, but a few spiders are dangerous, such as widow spiders, recluses and the Australian funnel-webs.
    • 🦑SquidFast-swimming molluscs with large eyes, eight arms and two long tentacles.
    • 🐾StingrayA sting is very painful but rarely fatal. Steve Irwin’s death in 2006 was only the second recorded in Australian waters since 1945.
    • 🐻Sun bearThe smallest bear, from the tropical forests of Southeast Asia, named for the orange to cream patch on its chest.
    • 🦢SwanMute swans can be very aggressive defending their nests, hissing and then striking with bony spurs on their wings and biting.
    • 🐟SwordfishA large, migratory predatory fish with a long, flat, pointed bill.
    • 🕷️TarantulaBites from New World tarantulas usually cause only local pain, but allergic reactions can be serious, and the hairs can damage eyes.
    • 🐾Tasmanian devilA dog-sized carnivorous marsupial, now found only on Tasmania, and the largest carnivorous marsupial since the thylacine died out in 1936.
    • 🐸ToadGlands behind the head hold a poison the toad releases when stressed. Some, like the cane toad, are much more toxic than others.
    • 🦇Vampire batThree species of bats from Central and South America, the only mammals that feed only on blood.
    • 🦭WalrusA huge Arctic pinniped with long ivory tusks, bristly whiskers and a nearly hairless body.
    • 🐗WarthogWarthogs use their razor-sharp lower tusks in fights and to defend themselves against predators.
    • 🐝WaspMany wasps can sting, and colonies defend their nests. People are stung most in late summer, when workers look for sugary food.
    • 🐗Wild boarThe wild pig of Eurasia and North Africa, now one of the widest-ranging mammals in the world after introductions to the Americas and Oceania.
    • 🐈WildcatThe European wildcat: bigger and stouter than a house cat, with longer striped fur, a dark stripe along the back and a bushy, black-tipped tail.
    • 🐾WildebeestTwo species of antelope from eastern and southern Africa, also called gnus: the blue wildebeest and the black wildebeest.
    • 🐺WolfAttacks on people are rare, and most recorded ones involved wolves with rabies.
    • 🐾WolverineThe largest land-dwelling member of the weasel family, a solitary, muscular carnivore of the far north.
    • 🦓ZebraZebras bite and kick hard, and can turn aggressive, especially when defending their young.
